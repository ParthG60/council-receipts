Read up on it

    Methodology

    What it is. Every English council in the Council Gateway (282), across the political spectrum. Pick one to see what it talks about, where its money goes, and how it compares.

    What they talk about. Meeting minutes come from the Council Gateway API (Poteris), covering October 2024 – March 2025, matched to the 2024-25 financial year so discussion and spend cover the same period. Each document is classified by keyword counting against a fixed taxonomy. A council's discussion share for a topic = that topic's keyword hits ÷ all topic hits across its minutes. Statutory notices (licensing, road closures) are filtered out.

    Where the money goes. Spend is from the MHCLG Revenue Outturn 2024-25, gross expenditure by service (staff + running costs), converted to £ per resident. Gross measures the resources a council actually pours into each service. The alternative, net expenditure (gross minus fees-and-charges income), measures the burden on council tax instead, and understates services that charge heavily: on a net basis Camden's £37m-a-year roads operation would show up as negative, because parking income (£57m) more than covers it. One caveat with gross: it includes activity funded by fees and charges as well as council tax. Bath's Roman Baths spend, for instance, is more than covered by admissions.

    Taxonomy

    Scorecard. The English Indices of Deprivation 2025 (IoD2025, MHCLG): overall deprivation (IMD) plus seven domains — Income, Employment, Education & skills, Health & disability, Crime, Barriers to housing & services, Living environment. Ranks and deciles sit within tier: districts/unitaries are ranked out of 296, county councils out of 153 — never compare a rank or decile across tiers. Decile 1 = most-deprived tenth, decile 10 = least-deprived tenth, within that pool. Score is the underlying IoD2025 value; higher score and lower decile both mean more deprived. Score and the national most-deprived-10% share are the only figures comparable across tiers, since they're set against all of England rather than within a pool.

    Limitations. Control is the current composition of each council, from Open Council Data UK (public domain) — not a historical snapshot. Deprivation is England-only: there's no directly comparable UK-wide index. Some councils have no matched Gateway minutes for FY2024-25, so their discussion panels are omitted. Keyword classification is crude: it counts what minutes mention, not what was decided.

    What's next

    This started as a one-day build at Campaign Lab, so there's a lot I still want to improve. Here's roughly what I'm working towards, in order:

    1. Every English council. Growing from the councils here to every English council in the Council Gateway. Already underway in the background.
    2. A richer scorecard. Five metrics is what fit in a day. I'd like more of them, and better-chosen ones, so the comparison is fairer.
    3. Voting outcomes. Not just what a council discusses, but what it actually decides, and who voted which way.
    4. Financial health. Reserves, debt servicing, and Section 114 (effective bankruptcy) risk, so you can see which councils are under real strain.

    I'd also genuinely value your take on any of these:

    • Anything that's confusing, missing, or looks off.
    • Charts that could be clearer or easier to read at a glance.
    • Datasets or metrics worth folding in.
    • Questions you'd want this to answer but it doesn't yet.
