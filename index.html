Read up on it

    Methodology

    What it is. English councils across the political spectrum — pick one, see what it talks about, where its money goes, and how it compares.

    What they talk about. Meeting minutes come from the Council Gateway API (Poteris), covering October 2024 – March 2025 — deliberately matched to the 2024-25 financial year so discussion and spend cover the same period. Each document is classified by keyword counting against a fixed taxonomy. A council's discussion share for a topic = that topic's keyword hits ÷ all topic hits across its minutes. Statutory notices (licensing, road closures) are filtered out — they're procedural, not deliberation.

    Where the money goes. Spend is from the MHCLG Revenue Outturn 2024-25, gross expenditure by service (staff + running costs), converted to £ per resident. Gross measures the resources a council actually pours into each service. The alternative — net expenditure, i.e. gross minus fees-and-charges income — measures the burden on council tax instead, and understates activity wherever charging is heavy: on a net basis Camden's £37m-a-year roads operation would show up as negative, because parking income (£57m) more than covers it. The trade-off cuts the other way too: gross includes activity funded by fees and charges, not just your council tax — Bath's Roman Baths spend, for instance, is more than paid for by admissions.

    Taxonomy

    Scorecard. GCSE Attainment 8 (DfE, 2023/24, higher is better), CO₂ per person (DESNZ, 2024, lower is better), rent affordability = rent ÷ pay (ONS/ASHE, lower is better), recorded crime per 1,000 (ONS/Home Office, lower is better), and — where landed — A-road delay in secs/vehicle-mile (DfT congestion data, lower is better). Heatmap colour and league-table sort are by rank among the councils on this site, not the raw value.

    Limitations. Bristol has no minutes in the Gateway before April 2026, so it's excluded from discussion panels for the matched year. Kent and Lancashire are county councils — they don't run every service, and some scorecard figures are county-wide proxies (flagged where used). Recorded crime reflects reporting as well as crime. Keyword classification is crude — it measures what minutes mention, not the quality of debate. Discussion (Oct 2024–Mar 2025) predates some councils' 2025/2026 elections, so their talk reflects the administration in office at the time — the comparison against the same-year spend stays internally consistent.
