Methodology

What it is. Covers 283 English local authorities: the 282 in the Council Gateway, plus Kensington and Chelsea (outcomes, finance and contracts only — it has no minutes in the source so no talk-vs-spend). Pick any council to explore what it talks about, where its money goes, and how it compares on key outcome metrics.

What they talk about. Meeting minutes come from the Council Gateway API (Poteris). Each document is classified by keyword counting against a fixed 10-topic taxonomy. A council's discussion share for a topic = that topic's keyword hits ÷ all topic hits across its minutes. Statutory notices (licensing orders, road closures, traffic regulation orders) are filtered out.

Where the money goes. Spend is from the MHCLG Revenue Outturn 2024-25, gross expenditure by service (staff + running costs), converted to £ per resident. Gross measures the resources a council actually pours into each service. One caveat: it includes activity funded by fees and charges as well as council tax.

Spend benchmarks. England spend figures are calculated as population-weighted England averages across all 283 local authorities (total English spend divided by total population for £/resident, and total topic spend divided by total expenditure for budget shares).

Taxonomy

Quality of Life scorecard & league table. Eight outcome-based indicators with national ranks across England (Rank #1 = best; Rank #283 = worst):

  • Life Expectancy at Birth: ONS Health State Life Expectancies (2025, 2021–23 pool). England average: 81.9 yrs.
  • GCSE Attainment 8: DfE Key Stage 4 performance (2023/24), average points per pupil across 8 GCSE subjects. England average: 46.1 pts.
  • Rent Affordability: ONS PIPR private rents ÷ ASHE median gross full-time earnings (a single full-time earner, not household income — which is why this reads higher than ONS's household-based ~30% figure). England average: 43.5%.
  • Air Quality PM2.5: Defra / OHID Public Health Outcomes Framework, percentage of mortality attributable to fine particulate air pollution. England average: 5.29%.
  • Child Poverty: DWP Children in Low Income Families, percentage of children under 16 in relative low-income households. England average: 19.8%.
  • Claimant Rate: Nomis Claimant Count (July 2026), percentage of 16–64 population claiming unemployment benefits. England average: 4.0%.
  • Crime Rate: ONS Community Safety Partnerships (2024), recorded offences per 1,000 residents. England average: 85.3.
  • House / Earnings: HM Land Registry average house price ÷ ASHE median gross full-time annual pay (weekly × 52). Lower is more affordable. Ratio of a mean price to a median wage, so treat as an indicative affordability multiple, not an official ONS ratio.

Standout flags. The pills under each council name are generated from national benchmarks: top/bottom 20% on crime, rent affordability, child poverty and house price-to-earnings; top/bottom 10% on borrowing per resident; any active Section 114 notice or Exceptional Financial Support; the largest talk-vs-spend gap among discretionary services (statutory social care and schools excluded); and any change of political control since 2025.

Council tiers & remits. Shire Districts (lower-tier councils) deliver Housing, Local Planning, Waste Collection, and Leisure. Upper-tier County Councils provide Education, Adult & Children's Social Care, Public Health, and Highways. Unitary and London/Metropolitan Boroughs deliver all services under a single authority.

AI-native. Every council has a plain-text dossier at dossiers/{ONS code}.md and a one-click "copy citizen briefing" button, so you can point your own ChatGPT or Claude at verified numbers rather than a scraped page.

Limitations. Political control reflects current council composition from Open Council Data UK (2026). Some councils have incomplete Gateway minutes, so discussion panels are omitted while retaining finance and outcome metrics. Keyword classification counts what minutes mention, not debate quality or voting outcomes.

England's councils, by tier

Not every council does the same job. Comparing budgets across tiers is misleading — a shire district has no social care or education budget at all. These are the four kinds of authority in this build:

  • Single-tier (all services) — unitary authorities, metropolitan boroughs and London boroughs. One council runs everything: social care, education, housing, waste, highways, planning.
  • Shire district (lower tier) — district and borough councils inside a two-tier county area. They handle planning, housing, waste collection, leisure and licensing, but not social care, education or highways.
  • Shire county (upper tier) — county councils that cover the districts beneath them: social care, education, highways, libraries and waste disposal.
  • Combined & county combined authorities — strategic bodies (often with a directly elected mayor) covering transport, skills and economic growth across several councils; they are not in the 283 service-benchmark league.

Tier classification from the ONS / Open Council Data authority register. The league table and party comparisons default to single-tier authorities precisely because they are the only like-for-like group.

How we made this

Council Receipts joins official government returns to the actual record of council meetings, so you can compare what a council spends against what it discusses. Here is exactly what is official, what is derived, and what is machine-classified — plus how to flag a mistake.

Provenance, by type

  • Official return Published government statistics, used as-is. Figures are authoritative and traceable to the named release: MHCLG Revenue Outturn, MHCLG Borrowing, ONS population and quality-of-life statistics, DfE, DWP, Nomis.
  • Direct record Items published by councils or government as records of fact, presented with light formatting: Contracts Finder awards, Section 114 and EFS notices, Gateway meeting documents.
  • Algorithmic extraction Machine output that can be wrong. Discussion topics come from keyword classification of meeting minutes against a fixed 10-topic taxonomy. It counts what minutes mention, not debate quality or voting outcomes.
  • Derived A calculation we perform by joining two official sources (benchmarks, deciles, talk-vs-spend gaps). The inputs are official; the arithmetic is ours.

What you should treat with care

  • Discussion shares are algorithmic. A keyword hit is evidence a topic was mentioned, not that it was decided, funded or debated well.
  • Single-year money figures. Spend is the 2024-25 Revenue Outturn; borrowing is the latest published quarter. These are snapshots, not multi-year trends.
  • Contract values are as published. They may reflect framework ceilings, and multi-supplier framework lots are excluded.
  • Tiers are not comparable. Shire districts have no social-care or education budget at all, so England-wide rankings should always be read within a tier (use the tier toggles).
  • Some councils have incomplete minutes. Where Gateway coverage is thin, discussion panels are omitted and only finance and outcome metrics are shown.

Corrections

If a number looks wrong, tell us — every report is read, and confirmed fixes are corrected at source in the public build. Report a discrepancy, or open an issue on GitHub.
